Serious games for learning, training, and therapy

I design and study games that carry a job beyond entertainment — teaching a science concept, guiding a therapy exercise, screening for a developmental condition — and I test whether they actually do that job.

The work began with the opposite question. Between 2009 and 2011 I ran empirical studies on what makes an entertainment game enjoyable and how that quality can be evaluated at all, first at the University of Regina and then across a series of conference papers on game evaluation and mobile game interaction. Measurement came before purpose.

From 2014 the same methods moved into Thai classrooms. Working with the Faculty of Education at Khon Kaen University, my students and I built context-aware ubiquitous learning systems and pedagogy-embedded games for primary and secondary science, covering nutrition, vitamins, and physical concepts, and tested them with real cohorts rather than in a lab.

Since 2019 the focus has been special education and therapy. With autistic children in Thailand as the primary participants, we have built screening applications, an interactive system for physical education class, a sound interaction framework, and a breathing interaction system for self-regulation. That line of work produced a book chapter on serious games for special education in 2023 and, in 2026, a design framework that connects all three uses — learning, training, and therapy — into a single process.

I teach game design, software engineering, interactive art programming, and ubiquitous computing. The prototypes below are teaching material: small, single-purpose arithmetic games that students can take apart and rebuild.

  1. Challenge
  2. Player action
  3. Feedback
  4. Measured outcome

Every game here runs that loop. The fourth step is what separates a serious game from a fun one — the outcome is recorded, it sets the next challenge, and it is the thing the study design is built around.

Ten arithmetic prototypes

Each one takes a single arithmetic skill and wraps it in one mechanic — no menus, no accounts, nothing to install. They run in the browser and are meant to be read as source as much as played. Work in progress; titles and topics will change as each is finished.
